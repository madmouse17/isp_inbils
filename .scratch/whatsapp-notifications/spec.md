## Owner decisions

1. **Q1 — Provider.** Facts: no WhatsApp code exists in inbils (grep `whatsapp` in `app Modules config routes` finds only the comment at `app/Listeners/ReportFailedQueueJob.php:40`); the roadmap row lists only Fonnte/Wablas/Twilio (`.scratch/isp-roadmap/spec.md:12`).
   (a) **WhatsApp Cloud API direct (Meta, official).** Needs a verified Meta Business portfolio, a WhatsApp Business Account, a dedicated number not registered on the WhatsApp app, and every business-initiated message as a pre-approved template. Meta charges per *delivered* message by market × category; service messages and utility messages sent in response to a user inside the 24 h window are free; volume tiers lower utility rates (whatsappbusiness.com/products/platform-pricing, read 2026-10-11; the owner reads the current IDR rate for Indonesia × utility there). No reseller markup. Ban risk only from message quality (users blocking/reporting), not from the connection. Delivery/read/failed status by signed webhook.
   (b) **Official BSP** (Indonesian: Qontak by Mekari; global: Twilio, Gupshup). Same Meta templates, policy and per-message price plus the BSP's fee; adds local onboarding help, IDR invoicing and a dashboard. Each BSP has its own API shape, so the client code is BSP-specific.
   (c) **Unofficial gateways (Fonnte, Wablas).** Automate WhatsApp Web on an ordinary number scanned by QR; flat monthly fee, free-form text, no template approval. Fonnte states the service is unofficial (fonnte.com FAQ); WhatsApp bans accounts that use unofficial apps (faq.whatsapp.com "About account bans for unofficial apps"). Losing the number cuts every customer channel at once; message content and customer numbers pass through a third party with no Meta data terms; delivery status is best-effort.
   **Recommend (a).** Billing reminders and receipts are exactly Meta's "utility" category, and an ISP cannot afford its customer-facing number being banned. Tradeoff: Meta Business verification and template approval take days before the first real message; per-message cost instead of a flat fee. Fallback if verification stalls: (b) Qontak; only the client class changes (Q2).

## Problem Statement

Customers of the ISP read WhatsApp, not e-mail. Today they learn about a new invoice, an upcoming due date, a received payment, the progress of their complaint or a technician visit only if they open an e-mail or call the office. Bills go overdue because nobody reminded them, the call centre answers "has my payment arrived?" and "when is the technician coming?" all day, and technicians learn about new SPKs only inside the app.

## Solution

When a customer has agreed to WhatsApp notifications, the system sends short, approved WhatsApp template messages from the company's official WhatsApp Business number: invoice issued; reminders three days and one day before and one day after the due date while a balance remains; payment received; ticket created / being handled / resolved; and the technician visit date. Technicians receive their SPK assignment and visit date on WhatsApp. New customer accounts get a WhatsApp notice that their set-password link is in their e-mail. Staff record consent on the customer form and see each message's delivery status on the customer page; the company admin turns the feature and each event on or off, sets quiet hours and a monthly cap. Customers stop all messages by replying STOP.

## User Stories

1. As a customer, I want a WhatsApp message when my invoice is issued, with number, amount, due date and a portal link, so that I know what to pay.
2. As a customer, I want a reminder three days and one day before the due date while I still owe money, so that I pay on time.
3. As a customer, I want a reminder one day after the due date if I have not paid, so that I can avoid suspension.
4. As a customer who already paid, I want no reminders for that invoice, so that I am not harassed.
5. As a customer, I want a confirmation with the amount and remaining balance when my payment is recorded, whether I paid online or at the counter, so that I trust the payment arrived.
6. As a customer, I want a message with the ticket code when my complaint is registered, so that I can refer to it.
7. As a customer, I want to know when a technician takes my ticket and when it is resolved, so that I do not have to call.
8. As a customer, I want to know the visit date and the technician's name when an SPK is assigned for my site, so that someone is home.
9. As a customer, I want to receive messages only after I agreed, and to stop them by replying STOP, so that my consent is respected.
10. As a customer, I want no batch messages at night, so that I am not woken up.
11. As a new customer, I want a WhatsApp notice that my set-password link is in my e-mail, so that I find it.
12. As a technician, I want a WhatsApp message with the SPK code, visit date and a link to the SPK when I am assigned, so that I can plan my day.
13. As a customer service officer, I want to tick "customer agrees to WhatsApp notifications" when registering or editing a customer, with the date and who recorded it kept, so that consent is provable.
14. As a customer service officer, I want to untick consent when a customer asks by phone, so that messages stop immediately.
15. As a customer service officer, I want to see on the customer page the last messages with event, time and status (sent, delivered, read, failed, skipped and why), so that I can answer "I never got it".
16. As a customer service officer, I want to see when a customer's phone is not a valid Indonesian mobile number, so that I can fix it.
17. As a company admin, I want WhatsApp off by default and a switch per event, so that we roll out gradually.
18. As a company admin, I want to set quiet hours in our local time, so that batch messages respect customers.
19. As a company admin, I want a monthly message cap and a counter of sent, delivered, failed and billable messages, so that cost stays predictable.
20. As an owner, I want the feature disabled when no WhatsApp credentials are configured, so that nothing is sent by accident.
21. As an operator, I want a failed send retried automatically and a permanently failing one marked failed with the error code, so that transient outages do not lose messages.
22. As an operator, I want retries, re-runs of the reminder command and duplicate webhooks never to send a message twice, so that customers are not spammed.
23. As an operator, I want forged webhook calls rejected without database changes, so that nobody can fake delivery or opt customers out.
24. As an auditor, I want no phone numbers, message text or tokens in logs, failed jobs or the message table, so that customer data does not leak.
25. As a customer of company B, I never receive a message about company A's records, and company A's staff never see company B's message log.
26. As a developer, I want every WhatsApp send to pass through one channel with one consent/quiet-hours/cap/idempotency gate, so that rules live in one place.

## Implementation Decisions

- **Channel and client (core).** A WhatsApp notification channel class plus one Cloud API client on the `Http` client (timeout 10 s, connect timeout 5 s, bearer token and Graph version from config). The client also owns webhook signature verification. The channel is the single gate, in this order: feature configured → company `whatsapp_enabled` and event enabled → recipient resolved (customer consent present and not withdrawn; technician active with an active employee profile) → phone normalised (Q7) → monthly cap (Q12) → idempotency key free → send template → store provider message id. Every non-send outcome writes a `skipped` row with a reason (`disabled`, `event_disabled`, `no_consent`, `opted_out`, `invalid_phone`, `budget_exceeded`). A key already used by a non-`skipped` row is a silent no-op; a `skipped` row is overwritten by a later attempt with the same key. The channel runs inside `CompanyService::runAs(notification company id)`.
- **Notification contract.** Each WhatsApp-capable notification carries only `companyId` and the subject id (plus a reminder stage where relevant). `toWhatsApp(notifiable)` returns, resolved at send time:

  ```php
  // array shape, not a class
  ['event' => 'invoice_reminder', 'template' => 'tagihan_pengingat', 'language' => 'id',
   'parameters' => ['PT Contoh Net', 'INV-2026-00012', 'Rp 250.000', '14 Okt 2026', 'https://inbils.example/portal/invoices/12'],
   'subject_type' => 'invoice', 'subject_id' => 12, 'dedupe_key' => 'invoice_reminder:12:H-3:2026-10-14',
   'batch' => true] // batch => quiet hours apply
  ```

  It sets `afterCommit()`, `viaQueues()` mapping the channel class to queue `whatsapp`, `withDelay()` for quiet hours on batch events, `middleware()` with the per-company `RateLimited` limiter, `tries` 5 and backoff `[60, 300, 900, 3600]`. Permanent provider errors throw a non-retryable exception that marks the row `failed`.
- **Recipients.** `Customer` becomes notifiable (WhatsApp route = `customers.phone`). Technicians are `User`s; their route is `employeeProfile.phone`. The access-link notice routes the customer user to its linked customer (`User::customer()`).
- **Schema.** New `whatsapp_messages` (company_id FK, recipient_type, recipient_id, event, subject_type, subject_id, template, language, dedupe_key, status enum `queued|sent|delivered|read|failed|skipped`, skip_reason, error_code, provider_message_id unique nullable, recipient_hash (HMAC-SHA256 with the app key), recipient_last4, billable nullable bool, pricing_category nullable, sent_at, delivered_at, read_at, failed_at, timestamps; unique `(company_id, dedupe_key)`; indexes `(company_id, recipient_type, recipient_id, created_at)` and `(company_id, created_at)`), `BelongsToCompany`, `Prunable` after 12 months. `customers` gains `whatsapp_opt_in_at`, `whatsapp_opt_in_source` (`staff`), `whatsapp_opt_in_by` (nullable FK users, null on delete), `whatsapp_opt_out_at`; not mass-assignable. Both migrations reversible.
- **Dedupe keys.** `invoice_issued:{invoice}`, `invoice_reminder:{invoice}:{H-3|H-1|H+1}:{due_date}`, `payment_received:{payment}`, `ticket_{created|assigned|resolved}:{ticket}`, `spk_scheduled:{work_order}:{technician user id}` (technician), `spk_scheduled_customer:{work_order}` (customer), `access_link_notice:{user}:{Y-m-d-H}` (at most one notice per hour).
- **Config.** `services.whatsapp` block (token, phone number id, business account id, app secret, verify token, graph version); feature enabled iff token and phone number id are present. Template map (event → template name, language `id`, parameter order, batch flag) in a `whatsapp` config file. Per-company non-secret settings in `companies.settings`: `whatsapp_enabled` bool, `whatsapp_events` list of event keys, `whatsapp_quiet_start`/`whatsapp_quiet_end` `H:i`, `whatsapp_monthly_cap` int 0-100000; validated explicitly in the settings request.
- **Triggers** (all after commit, ids only): invoice issued at the recurring batch insert and in `send`; payment received in `recordPayment` (covers manual and Midtrans); reminders by a new `billing:whatsapp-reminders {--company=} {--dry-run}` per active company via `runAs`, scheduled daily 01:00 UTC with `withoutOverlapping()`, selecting invoices `sent|partial|overdue` with `paid_amount < total` whose due date is today+3, today+1 or today−1 in the company timezone; ticket created / first assigned / resolved for tickets with a customer; SPK assignment to the technician on every new technician and to the customer on first assignment with a scheduled date; access-link notice added to `CustomerAccountCreated::via` when the user has a linked customer (consent checked by the channel).
- **Webhook.** `GET|POST /webhooks/whatsapp`, outside the `web` group, no CSRF, throttled for invalid signatures only (Midtrans pattern). Status updates are monotonic (`read` never goes back to `delivered`; `failed` only from `queued|sent`); unknown message ids are ignored with 200; inbound `STOP`/`BERHENTI` (case-insensitive, trimmed) sets `whatsapp_opt_out_at` on every customer whose normalised phone matches, across companies, audit-logged per company.
- **Staff surfaces.** Consent is written only by one dedicated action (pattern: the acquirer action called from the onboarding action), invoked from customer onboarding and customer update; gate = the existing `customer.create`/`customer.update`. Unticking sets `whatsapp_opt_out_at`; re-ticking sets a new `opt_in_at` and clears opt-out. Customer show (gate `customer.view`, already required by the page): consent state, phone reachability, last 20 message rows (event, subject, status, reason, time, masked phone `••••1234`). Company settings (`company.manage` to edit, `viewSettings` to see): WhatsApp section (switch, events, quiet hours, cap) plus this month's sent/delivered/failed/billable counts and whether credentials are configured (boolean only).
- **Audit.** Log name `whatsapp` for consent changes and STOP opt-outs (ids only).
- **No new dependency** under Q2a; under Q1b/Q2b the owner approves the package and version first.
